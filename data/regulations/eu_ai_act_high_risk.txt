EU AI Act high-risk banking model update.

TRAINING_DATA. Providers and deployers of high-risk AI systems used for
creditworthiness, pricing, fraud detection, or customer eligibility decisions
must establish training data governance controls over training datasets,
validation datasets, and testing datasets so that the training data used to
build high-risk AI models is relevant, representative, accurate, and complete,
with documented dataset curation, labelling, and sampling practices.

DATA_LINEAGE. The bank must maintain documented data lineage and provenance
for every dataset used to train high-risk AI systems, capturing source systems,
upstream ingestion points, transformation history, data flow across pipelines,
quality checks, approvals, and end-to-end lineage evidence retained for
regulatory review.

LOGGING_MONITORING. High-risk AI systems shall automatically produce event
logs and continuous monitoring records throughout the model lifecycle so that
inference decisions, model outputs, human interventions, configuration changes,
and system errors are captured in centrally aggregated logs available for audit
and supervisory investigation.

AI_GOVERNANCE. The bank must operate an AI governance framework that provides
effective human oversight of high-risk AI decisions, including a designated AI
governance committee, documented escalation paths, defined authority to
override automated outcomes, and evidence that reviewers understand model
limitations and approved use cases.

MODEL_RISK. The bank must manage model risk for high-risk AI systems through
independent model validation, backtesting, benchmark comparisons, drift
detection, and ongoing performance monitoring, and must document model risk
remediation whenever model performance, stability, or accuracy falls below
approved model risk tolerance thresholds.

AUDITABILITY. Providers must establish and maintain a risk management system
as a continuous, iterative process across the entire lifecycle of the
high-risk AI system, and must produce auditable records covering risk
identification, estimation of foreseeable misuse, evaluation of post-market
monitoring data, and residual-risk sign-off so that internal audit and
competent authorities can trace each decision through a complete audit trail.

METADATA. The bank must draw up and keep up to date technical documentation
and system metadata for each high-risk AI system before it is placed on the
market, cataloguing in a central metadata repository the general description
of the system, its elements and development process, monitoring and control
metadata, risk management measures, and any lifecycle changes, so competent
authorities can discover and assess the catalogued system.

CYBER. Providers must design and develop high-risk AI systems with an
appropriate level of cybersecurity and declare the relevant performance
metrics in the accompanying instructions of use. Systems must be resilient
against cyber attacks by unauthorised third parties attempting to alter their
use, outputs, or performance by exploiting vulnerabilities such as data
poisoning, model poisoning, adversarial examples, prompt injection, or
confidentiality attacks.

CONDUCT. Deployers of high-risk AI systems used for creditworthiness or
insurance pricing of natural persons must uphold fair customer conduct by
carrying out a fundamental rights impact assessment prior to first use,
describing categories of natural persons likely to be affected, specific
risks of harm to consumers, human oversight measures, and remediation should
those conduct risks materialise, and must notify the market surveillance
authority of the results.

REG_REPORTING. High-risk AI systems must be registered in the EU database for
high-risk AI systems before being placed on the market or put into service,
and the registration must be kept up to date as part of the provider's and
deployer's regulatory reporting obligations to competent authorities and
market surveillance authorities.

INCIDENT_MGMT. Where a high-risk AI system presents a risk to health, safety,
or fundamental rights, or where a serious incident occurs, the provider must
trigger its incident management process, investigate immediately, inform the
affected deployers and, where applicable, the notified body, and report the
serious incident to the market surveillance authorities of the Member States
concerned no later than 15 days after becoming aware of it, or immediately
and not later than 2 days in case of widespread infringement or death of a
person.

THIRD_PARTY_RISK. Providers established outside the Union whose high-risk AI
systems are placed on the Union market must, by written mandate, appoint an
authorised representative established in the Union before making their
systems available, and the bank must apply third-party risk management
controls, due diligence, and contractual oversight to that representative and
to any subcontractors or downstream providers acting on the provider's behalf.

RETENTION. The provider and the authorised representative must apply a
records retention period of at least 10 years after the high-risk AI system
has been placed on the market, retaining the EU declaration of conformity,
the technical documentation, logs required by law, and post-market monitoring
records at the disposal of competent authorities in accordance with the
retention schedule.

ACCESS_CONTROL. The bank must ensure that natural persons assigned to human
oversight of high-risk AI systems are granted the access rights, privileged
permissions, and role-based authority required to correctly interpret the
system's output, to decide not to use the system, to disregard, override, or
reverse the output, and to intervene in the operation of the system or
interrupt it through a stop button or equivalent access control mechanism.

AI_GOVERNANCE. Providers and deployers must take measures to ensure, as part
of ongoing AI governance and workforce competence, a sufficient level of AI
literacy for their staff and other persons dealing with the operation and use
of high-risk AI systems on their behalf, taking into account their technical
knowledge, experience, education, training, and the context in which the AI
systems are to be used.
